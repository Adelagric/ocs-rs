\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A companion note to the support-first solver. For genomic optimum contribution selection
(OCS) we bracket the size of the optimal support: an existential $n$-independent total-support bound holds without effective caps in the
no-ridge limit (Theorem~\ref{thm:eps0}), no universal bound survives the ridge
(Theorem~\ref{thm:noridge}), and the practical regime between them is governed by the joint
geometry of spectrum, breeding values and cap, with no reliable scalar predictor among those tested. A conditional KKT identity
(Proposition~\ref{prop:kkt}) describes free contributions. Historical experiments are retained with context; scripts and prerequisites are listed below.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}

Genomic OCS solves, for $n$ candidates with breeding values $\bb \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and
relationship matrix $\bG = \bZ\bZ\tp/s + \varepsilon\mathbf{I}$ ($\bZ$ the $n\times m$ centred
genotype matrix, $s$ a scale, $\varepsilon$ a small ridge for positive-definiteness),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ocs}
  \max\ \bb\tp\bc \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \bA\bc = \bd,\ \ \mathbf{0} \le \bc \le \bu,\ \ \bc\tp\bG\bc \le k,
\end{equation}
where $\bA$ is the $q\times n$ budget matrix ($q=1$ for the simplex $\bone\tp\bc=1$; $q=2$ for
the sexed form $\sum_{\text{males}} = \sum_{\text{females}} = \tfrac12$). Empirically the
optimum $\bc^\star$ activates very few candidates: its support $S = \{i : c^\star_i > 0\}$ is
$\approx15$--$30$ in historical loose-cap real-panel runs. At the manuscript's operational contribution-proxy points it is tens to hundreds (mouse: 47/89/182 at $\Delta F=2/1/0.5\%$); the structured $n$-sweep gives roughly 80--103 at a fixed proxy point. These observations do not prove a universal bound. What bounds
$|S|$?

The answer is a \emph{bracket}. In the uncapped no-ridge case there is an existential $n$-independent bound
(\S\ref{sec:eps0}); once the ridge is present there is provably no universal bound, the
support reaching the whole population (\S\ref{sec:noridge}); and the practical regime between
them is set by the joint geometry of $(\text{spectrum}, \bb, k)$, whose empirical map shows no reliable scalar predictor among those tested (\S\ref{sec:empirical}). A conditional KKT identity
(\S\ref{sec:kkt}) describes free contributions.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon = 0$}{eps=0} bound}\label{sec:eps0}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:eps0}
Assume $\varepsilon=0$, $s>0$, nonempty feasibility, and simplex or sex-budget equalities so that $\{\bA\bc=\bd,\ \bc\ge0\}$ is bounded. With no effective upper caps, there exists an optimum with
\[
 |S|\le\operatorname{rank}\!\begin{bmatrix}\bA\\\bZ\tp\\\bb\tp\end{bmatrix}\le q+r+1,
 \qquad r=\operatorname{rank}(\bZ)\le m.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with any attained optimum $\bc^\star$ and fix its image and objective in
\[
 P=\{\bc\ge0:\ \bA\bc=\bd,\ \bZ\tp\bc=\bZ\tp\bc^\star,\ \bb\tp\bc=\bb\tp\bc^\star\}.
\]
Every point has the same kinship $\|\bZ\tp\bc^\star\|^2/s$ and objective, hence is optimal. $P$ is nonempty and bounded. Let $M=[\bA;\bZ\tp;\bb\tp]$, of rank at most $q+r+1$. If a vertex had more than $\operatorname{rank}(M)$ positive coordinates, there would be a nonzero null direction supported on those coordinates. Two sufficiently small opposite perturbations preserve the equalities and nonnegativity, contradicting extremality. A vertex proves the bound. No duality or constraint qualification is required.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Finite-cap extension.} Intersect $P$ with $\bc\le\bu$. A vertex has at most $\operatorname{rank}(M)$ \emph{free} coordinates $F=\{i:0<c_i<u_i\}$, by the same perturbation argument. Positive upper-bound coordinates $U_+=\{i:c_i=u_i>0\}$ also count, so $|S|=|F|+|U_+|\le\operatorname{rank}(M)+|U_+|$. Arbitrary finite caps preclude an $n$-independent total-support bound: $q=1$, $r=0$, $\bG=0$, $k=0$, $u_i=2/n$ force $|S|\ge\lceil n/2\rceil$, although $q+r+1=2$. Strictly decreasing $\bb$ attains that minimum at an optimal capped LP vertex.

The theorem is an existence result about a vertex of an \emph{optimal slice}. The implemented active-set algorithm is not proved to return that vertex; no categorical conclusion about sparsity of interior-point or ADMM outputs follows. Enforcing the guarantee on arbitrary output would require separate vertex extraction.

\section{No universal bound for \texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon > 0$}{eps>0}}\label{sec:noridge}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:noridge}
For $\varepsilon>0$, no total-support bound $f(q,r)$ independent of $n$ exists, even without effective upper caps: the support can equal $n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (counterexample)]
Take the abstract OCS instance $q=1$, $\bG=\varepsilon\mathbf{I}$, $r=0$, $u_i=1$. This algebraic counterexample is not a usable marker panel for a public entry point. On the simplex $\|\bc\|^2\ge1/n$ by Cauchy--Schwarz. If any coordinate is zero, $\|\bc\|^2\ge1/(n-1)$. Thus for
\[
 \varepsilon/n<k<\varepsilon/(n-1),
\]
the uniform plan is strictly kinship-feasible, while every feasible plan, and hence every optimum for any $\bb$, has full support. This holds for arbitrary $n\ge2$.
\end{proof}

The historical sweep (\texttt{bound\_validation.py}, block 7, $n=300$) reported
\[|S|=300,295,256,151,52,12,\]
as $k$ relaxed from $1.05\times$ to $50\times$ the minimum. These are observations for one $\bb$; $1.05\times$ is outside the analytic full-support interval and is not a universal witness. Block 8 checks that interval and the finite-cap obstruction directly.

Here $\sum c_i^2$ measures concentration of contributions. With $N_e=1/\sum c_i^2$, $\Delta F=1/(2N_e)$ is a contribution-based proxy, not a measured rate of realised genomic inbreeding. Small observed support depends on the joint instance geometry, rather than a worst-case guarantee.

\section{A conditional KKT identity}\label{sec:kkt}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kkt}
Suppose $\varepsilon>0$ and an optimum admits KKT multipliers with $\lambda>0$ for kinship. On the free coordinates $F=\{i:0<c_i^\star<u_i\}$,
\[
 c_i^\star=\alpha b_i+\bw\tp\bze_i+v\tp a_i,\qquad i\in F,
\]
where $a_i$ is column $i$ of $\bA$. For sex-incidence budgets $v\tp a_i=\beta_{\mathrm{sex}(i)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $\ell,h\ge0$ be the lower and upper multipliers. Stationarity and complementarity are
\[
 \bb=\bA\tp\mu+2\lambda\bG\bc^\star-\ell+h,\qquad
 \ell_i c_i^\star=0,\quad h_i(u_i-c_i^\star)=0,\quad
 \lambda(\bc^{\star\top}\bG\bc^\star-k)=0.
\]
On $F$, both bound multipliers vanish. With $\by=\bZ\tp\bc^\star$ and $\bG\bc^\star=\bZ\by/s+\varepsilon\bc^\star$, division by $2\lambda\varepsilon$ gives $\alpha=1/(2\lambda\varepsilon)$, $\bw=-\by/(s\varepsilon)$, $v=-\mu/(2\lambda\varepsilon)$.
\end{proof}

The identity uses $m+q+1$ coefficients ($m+2$ for simplex, $m+3$ for two sex budgets). Active kinship alone does not imply $\lambda>0$. Existence of multipliers needs a suitable constraint qualification or a supplied certificate, and is assumed here. The identity does not apply to upper-bound coordinates and proves no cardinality bound. Taking $\varepsilon\to0$ cannot recover Theorem~\ref{thm:eps0}: multipliers and supports may change or diverge, and stationarity holds only on the current free coordinates. The slice proof is independent.

\section{The empirical regime between the theorems}\label{sec:empirical}

Between the two clean statements lies the regime breeders use, mapped across synthetic spectra,
$\bb$-alignments and caps and on the real panels:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$\bb$'s alignment with the spectrum drives $|S|$, inversely.} With $\bG$ and $k$
  fixed, putting $\bb$ on the dominant (coancestry-expensive) eigendirection forces a large
  support (mean $|S| \approx 146$ of $n = 800$ for $\bb$ the top eigenvector); spreading $\bb$
  across many cheap directions collapses it to $\approx 4$. Gain sought in an expensive
  direction must be diluted over many candidates to respect the cap (\texttt{bound\_balign.py}).
\item \textbf{No single scalar predicts $|S|$ across regimes.} Neither $\operatorname{rank}(\bG_0)$,
  the effective rank / participation ratio of the spectrum, the directional cost
  $\bb\tp\bG\bb/\bb\tp\bb$, nor the support of the unconstrained direction $(\bG^{-1}\bb)_+$
  tracks $|S|$ across both synthetic and real instances; the best dimensionless combination,
  $|S| \sim (\bb\tp\bG\bb/\bb\tp\bb \cdot 1/k)^{0.8}$, reaches only $R^2 \approx 0.58$ and
  mispredicts the real panels' relative support (\texttt{bound\_predictor.py},
  \texttt{bound\_lawfit.py}).
\item \textbf{The curve $|S|(k)$ is a per-instance power law of non-universal exponent.}
  $|S| \sim k^{-\alpha}$ fits each instance well ($R^2 \approx 0.9$--$0.98$ where the support is
  well resolved), with $\alpha \approx 1$ on both real panels but ranging $0.5$--$1.7$ across
  synthetic spectra and alignments (\texttt{bound\_curve.py}).
\item \textbf{Real panels.} Wheat ($n = 599$) and mouse ($n = 1814$): $|S| = 25/26$ and $17/26$
  (simplex / sexed-optiSel) at the historical loose cap. These two fixed panels do not establish stability in $n$; operational targets in the manuscript have larger supports
  (\texttt{bound\_real.py}).
\end{itemize}

Theorem~\ref{thm:noridge} rules out a universal bound in $q,r$ alone. The empirical sweeps separately show that the scalar predictors tested here do not reliably predict support across regimes.

\section{A conditional bound: the obstruction and the conjecture}

A conditional bound remains open. If the kinship constraint is \emph{redundant on the whole budget polytope}, an uncapped LP admits an optimal vertex with $|S|\le\operatorname{rank}(\bA)\le q$; finite caps instead give only $|F|\le\operatorname{rank}(\bA)$. Slack kinship at one optimum is insufficient: for constant $\bb$, $\bG=\mathbf{I}$ and $1/n<k<1/(n-1)$, the uniform optimum is slack but every feasible optimum has full support. For exact rank $r$ and $\varepsilon=0$, Theorem~\ref{thm:eps0} gives the uncapped existential bound and its finite-cap extension bounds free coordinates.

A bound in terms of \emph{effective} rank --- the realistic ask, since real spectra decay --- does
not follow from the same argument, and the obstruction is precise. Theorem~\ref{thm:eps0} linearises
the optimal slice by pinning $\bZ\tp\bc$, the \emph{entire} range of $\bG_0$; for a full-rank $\bG$
one would have to pin all $n$ projections $u_i\tp\bc$, giving only the trivial $|S| \le q + n + 1$.
One cannot pin just the top-$d$ projections and drop the tail, because the tail contributes
$\bc\tp E\bc = \sum_{i>d} \lambda_i (u_i\tp\bc)^2$ --- a \emph{genuine quadratic}, not a low-rank term
--- so fixing it is not a linear constraint and the slice is not a polytope. The tail's curvature is
exactly what lets the support spread; effective rank alone cannot replace exact rank in this vertex count. A conditional bound
therefore needs a different (spectral-perturbation) argument.

Two qualitative conditions motivate a possible conditional theorem; they are not quantitatively specified or proved sufficient:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(A1) No large degenerate cheap plateau.} A block of many near-equal small eigenvalues is
  a cheap subspace the optimum spreads into; $\bG = \varepsilon\mathbf{I}$ is the extreme case, giving
  $|S| = n$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:noridge}). A smoothly decaying spectrum has no such plateau.
\item \textbf{(A2) $\bb$ avoids the dominant directions.} Even with a gap, $\bb$ on the top (expensive)
  eigenvector forces dilution and a large support (mean $|S| \approx 146$ of $800$ in the alignment
  sweep).
\end{itemize}
The pure-ridge counterexample and alignment sweep motivate (A1) and (A2), without proving necessity of these informal conditions. Under quantitative versions at a specified operating point we conjecture small support; the historical 15--30 values concern loose caps, not all real panels; the growth half
($|S|$ rising as $k$ approaches its feasible minimum) follows the
contribution-based $\Delta F=1/(2N_e)$ proxy, distinct from realised genomic inbreeding, in the context of the genetic contributions literature (Wray \& Thompson 1990;
Woolliams \& Bijma 2000). Proving it --- coupling the spectral gap with $\bb$'s projection onto the
top eigenspace, robust to the tail's curvature --- is the open problem, the meeting point of
optimisation (the perturbed-cone face) and quantitative genetics (effective lineages).

\textbf{An exact handle on the effective support.} Write the \emph{effective} number of contributors
as the participation ratio $\mathrm{PR}(\bc^\star) = 1/\lVert\bc^\star\rVert^2$ (it equals $|S|$ for
uniform contributions, less when uneven). At a positive active cap $k>0$ with $\bone\tp\bc^\star = 1$ an identity
holds: $\mathrm{PR}(\bc^\star) = R(\bc^\star)/k$, where
$R(\bc^\star) = \bc^{\star\top}\bG\bc^\star / \bc^{\star\top}\bc^\star$ is the optimum's own Rayleigh
quotient (since $R\lVert\bc^\star\rVert^2 = \bc^{\star\top}\bG\bc^\star = k$). Hence
$\mathrm{PR}(\bc^\star) \le \lambda_1/k$ for every such active positive cap, and a bound on \emph{effective} support reduces to bounding $R(\bc^\star)$ --- the directional coancestry cost the optimum is forced to incur. This is
where (A2) bites: the measured cases where $\bb$ avoids dominant directions have small $R(\bc^\star)$ (observed: $R \approx 0.02$--$0.4$ on the structured cases versus
$\lambda_1 \approx 45$--$143$, giving $\mathrm{PR} \approx 2$--$12$), whereas $\bG = \varepsilon\mathbf{I}$
forces $R = \varepsilon = \lambda_1$. At an active cap, $\mathrm{PR}=\varepsilon/k$ lies in
$(n-1,n)$ throughout the full-support interval $\varepsilon/n<k<\varepsilon/(n-1)$,
and approaches $n$ as $k$ decreases to $\varepsilon/n$. Total support is already $n$
throughout that interval; $\mathrm{PR}=n$ holds only for uniform contributions.
For example, $n=3$ and $k=5\varepsilon/12$ give $|S|=3$ but $\mathrm{PR}=12/5$.
The residual open piece is now sharp and
scalar --- bound $R(\bc^\star)$ a priori under (A1)+(A2). (Historically $|S|$ ran $\approx1.5$--$2.5\times\mathrm{PR}$ in these cases. In general $\mathrm{PR}\le|S|$, so an upper bound on PR does not bound raw cardinality without another assumption on contribution sizes.) Verified
in \texttt{bound\_kernel.py}.

\textbf{Attacking this residual.} For positive-definite $\bG$, the simplex relaxation without lower or upper bounds has, in the active-kinship case with $\lambda>0$, the Rayleigh-quotient expression
$R_{\mathrm{relax}} = (\beta\tp\bG^{-1}\beta)/(\beta\tp\bG^{-2}\beta)$ with $\beta = \bb - \mu\bone$ ---
a $\lambda$-average weighted by $\beta_i^2/\lambda_i^2$, so small under (A2). But it predicts
$R(\bc^\star)$ only when $\bc \ge 0$ is near-inactive: on the real panels the unconstrained optimum
carries large negative entries ($\min \approx -1$ to $-2.6$) and $R_{\mathrm{relax}}$ falls
$60$--$300\times$ below $R(\bc^\star)$. So \textbf{nonnegativity is the irreducible core} --- fittingly,
$|S|$ is the cardinality of a nonnegativity-constrained QP optimum, hard in general --- and a proof
must be genuinely $\bc \ge 0$-aware, not a relaxation shortcut. The clean a priori handle (the
relaxation formula) and the irreducible obstacle ($\bc \ge 0$) are now both identified.
